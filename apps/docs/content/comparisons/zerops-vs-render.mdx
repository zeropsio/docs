Zerops and Render are both PaaS platforms for deploying apps without managing raw servers. Render leans toward predictable, plan-based pricing. Zerops leans toward per-minute compute with no workspace fee and full Linux containers you can SSH into.

Where Render is genuinely stronger, this page says so.

## At a glance

|                         | Zerops                                          | Render                                                                                     |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Pricing model**       | Compute billed per minute, no workspace fee     | Flat workspace tier + separate compute                                                     |
| **Egress / bandwidth**  | Egress included up to project tier limits       | Reduced in April 2026 (Pro: 500 GB → 25 GB, Hobby: 100 GB → 5 GB)                         |
| **SSH into containers** | Yes, any runtime service                        | No, more restricted service model                                                          |

## What Render is

Render is a PaaS built around predictable, plan-based pricing rather than pure usage billing. It is a common choice for static sites, web services, and teams that want costs to map cleanly to a workspace tier.

## Pricing

As of **August 2026**:

Render restructured its pricing on **April 23, 2026**, replacing per-seat fees with flat workspace tiers (roughly $0 to $499/month depending on tier) plus separate per-second compute charges:

- Starter-tier compute starts around **$7/month** per service
- Standard-tier compute starts around **$25/month** per service
- That same update reduced included bandwidth substantially: Pro-tier included bandwidth dropped from **500 GB to 25 GB**, and Hobby from **100 GB to 5 GB**
- Render's free Postgres tier is **deleted after 30 days** with no grace period; their paid Postgres tiers do not expire

Zerops has no separate workspace fee layered on top of compute. You pay for the resources you run, billed per minute. Egress is included up to project tier limits.

:::tip Check current numbers
Workspace tiers, bandwidth allowances, and compute rates change. Confirm on Render's own pricing page before you decide.
:::

## Where Render is genuinely strong

- **Predictable monthly cost.** Most of the bill maps to a plan tier instead of raw usage, which is easier to forecast.
- **Static sites and simple services.** A good fit when the free tier (or a low workspace tier) covers what you need.
- **Familiar PaaS model.** Web services, background workers, and cron jobs in a well-understood shape.

## Where Zerops differs

### No workspace fee on top of compute

Zerops does not charge a separate workspace fee layered on top of compute. Billing is per-minute resource usage. Render's model is a flat workspace tier plus separate compute.

### No expiring database tiers

Render's free Postgres tier is deleted after 30 days with no grace period. Render's paid Postgres tiers do not expire. Zerops managed Postgres is also a paid service and does not expire, billed per minute like the rest of your resources. See [PostgreSQL on Zerops](/postgresql/overview).

### Full Linux containers with SSH

Every Zerops container is a full Linux environment via LXC. You can SSH into any running runtime service. Render uses a more locked-down service model without general SSH into production instances.

### Egress / bandwidth

Zerops includes egress up to the limits of your project tier (100 GB on the light core, 3 TB on the serious core), with overage billed separately. Render reduced its included bandwidth substantially in April 2026 (Pro from 500 GB to 25 GB, Hobby from 100 GB to 5 GB). Compare your real traffic profile against the current allowances on both sides.

## Quick answers

<FAQ>
  <FAQItem question="Is Zerops cheaper than Render?">
    It depends on the workload. Render uses workspace tiers plus per-second compute, which can be easier to predict month to month. Zerops has no workspace fee and bills compute per minute. Bandwidth-heavy apps should compare Render's reduced included allowances (as of April 2026) against Zerops egress for the same traffic.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="Does Render give you SSH into containers?">
    No. Render uses a more restricted service model. Zerops gives SSH into any running runtime service via full Linux containers.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="What happened to Render free Postgres?">
    Render's free Postgres tier is deleted after 30 days with no grace period. Render's paid Postgres tiers persist without expiring. Zerops Postgres is a paid managed service from the start and also does not expire.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="Did Render change bandwidth limits?">
    Yes. In April 2026, Render reduced included bandwidth substantially: Pro dropped from 500 GB to 25 GB, and Hobby from 100 GB to 5 GB. Zerops includes egress up to project tier limits with separate overage pricing. Confirm current numbers on both sides before deciding.
  </FAQItem>
  <FAQItem question="When should I pick Render over Zerops?">
    When you want month-to-month cost that maps mostly to a plan tier, or when a static site / simple service fits cleanly on Render's free or low workspace tiers.
  </FAQItem>
</FAQ>
